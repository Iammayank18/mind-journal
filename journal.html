Brain Dump

Get everything out of your head and onto the page. No grammar. No structure. No judgement. Just write whatever is there — even if it's "I don't know what to write."

⏱ Timed writing — 10 minutes
10:00
Write until the timer ends. Don't stop.
Stuck? Try:
Right now I'm thinking about... What's bothering me is... I keep coming back to... My body feels... I wish I could stop thinking about...
0 words
Why this works: Research by James Pennebaker (Harvard, 1997) found that 15 minutes of free emotional writing reduced anxiety, lowered stress hormones, and improved working memory. When thoughts stay in your head, they loop. Writing them down tells your brain: "stored — you can let go now."

Thought Reframe

Take one heavy thought and walk it through this process. You're not trying to think positive — you're trying to think accurately.

1. The thought as it is
Write it exactly as it appears in your head, unfiltered.
2. Is this fact or feeling?
A fact can be verified. A feeling is real but not always accurate. Which is this?
3. What would I tell a friend?
If your best friend had this exact thought, what would you tell them?
4. A more accurate thought
Write a version of this thought that is more balanced and truthful (not forced positivity).

Breathe

A 4-7-8 breathing exercise. One round takes under a minute. Activates your body's calm response.

Breathe
Ready when you are
4 counts in · 7 hold · 8 out
Why 4-7-8: The extended exhale (8 counts) activates the parasympathetic nervous system — your body's "rest and digest" mode. Even one round shifts your nervous system out of alert mode. Dr. Andrew Weil calls it "a natural tranquillizer."
